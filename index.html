Enter the patient. The tool returns, for each bioprosthesis, the labeled sizes with their published one-year effective orifice area (EOA), the predicted indexed EOA (EOAi = EOA / BSA), the expected one-year mean gradient, and the manufacturer external diameter. The smallest size reaching the EOAi floor is underlined. The tool does not take aortic root dimensions: compare the external diameter yourself with the sinuses of Valsalva and the sinotubular junction measured on preoperative CT.

BMI ≥ 30: body surface area overestimates the metabolic demand of obese patients; the 2021 VARC-3 consensus uses lower EOAi thresholds (0.70 moderate, 0.55 severe). Select the 0.70 floor if you follow that convention.
How to read the result. Red and yellow mark the evidence-based floors for patient-prosthesis mismatch, below which outcome is worse. Green means the floor is cleared. Blue (EOAi ≥ 1.0 cm²/m²) is shown because a recent analysis suggests continuing benefit above the conventional threshold, but whether an EOAi above 0.85 cm²/m² or a gradient below 10 mmHg improves outcome is not demonstrated; blue is not a target. How far above the floor to aim, and at what operative cost (annular or sinus enlargement), depends on age, activity, ventricular function, operative risk, and concomitant procedures.
Data. One-year EOA and mean gradient are patient-weighted means of published series stratified by labeled size (sources listed under each valve); between-study variability is substantial and echocardiographic methods were not standardized. External diameters are manufacturer specifications and were not independently verified. The Avalus Ultra will be added when size-specific one-year data are available.
Root check. A prosthesis can be seated in supra-annular position when its external diameter does not exceed the sinus of Valsalva diameter; the aorta can be closed over it when the sinotubular junction is not narrower than the prosthesis. When the sinus is too small, annular enlargement is needed; when only the sinotubular junction is too small, a supra-annular (non-coronary sinus) patch suffices. These are the authors' institutional rules, not validated thresholds.
